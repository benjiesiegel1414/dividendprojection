The Vanguard Dividend Appreciation ETF launched on April 21, 2006, and it's now one of the biggest dividend funds in the world, with more than $110 billion in it. If you've ever typed "VIG dividend calculator" into Google, you're probably asking the right question: not "how much does it pay today" but "how much will it pay me in 10 or 20 years."

Because today's number is small. At about 1.5%, $VIG yields more than $VOO but less than $VYM or $SCHD. The whole case for $VIG is growth. So let's look at the real numbers.

The amounts bounce around from quarter to quarter. That's normal for a stock fund. $VIG just passes through whatever its 340 or so companies paid during the quarter, and they don't all pay on the same calendar. Don't read too much into one quarter. Look at the full year.

When is the next VIG dividend?

Vanguard keeps $VIG on a steady rhythm, with ex-dates near the end of each quarter. The last three December ex-dates were December 21, 2023, December 23, 2024 and December 22, 2025. So the next one should be around December 21, 2026, with the cash landing just before Christmas.

The amount isn't known until Vanguard announces it. Recent December checks have been in the $0.87 to $0.92 range, but that's history, not a promise.

And please don't buy the day before the ex-date to "grab" the dividend. The share price drops by about the dividend amount on the ex-date. You don't make money, you just turn part of your investment into a taxable payment.

Here's the part most people miss. The yield stays low partly because the share price keeps going up too. If a fund's dividend grows 6% a year and the price grows faster, the yield shrinks even though your income is rising. That's a good problem to have.

What VIG holds (and why the yield is so low)

$VIG tracks the S&P U.S. Dividend Growers Index. The rules are what make it different:

  • A company needs at least 10 straight years of dividend increases to get in.
  • The highest-yielding 25% of those eligible companies are cut out. That's on purpose: very high yields are often a sign of trouble.
  • REITs are excluded, and what's left is weighted by market cap, with a cap on any single stock at each rebalance.

Are VIG dividends qualified? How they're taxed

For the most part, yes. $VIG holds U.S. common stocks and skips REITs, so the large majority of its dividends usually count as qualified dividends. In a taxable account those are taxed at long-term capital gains rates (0%, 15% or 20% depending on income), as long as you've held the shares more than 60 days around the ex-date.

Vanguard publishes the exact qualified percentage every tax season, and your 1099-DIV is the final word. I walk through the same question for a close cousin in are DGRO dividends qualified.

Low yield plus qualified dividends also means a small tax bill while you're building. Most of your return comes as price growth that you don't pay tax on until you sell. For a taxable account, that's a real advantage over a T-bill fund like $SGOV or a covered call fund, where most of the income is taxed every year.

My take: who VIG is for

I've been a dividend investor for over a decade, and the thing I wish I'd understood earlier is that the yield you start with matters less than the income you end with. $VIG is built around that idea.

  • You have a long runway: 10, 20, 30 years before you need the income. Growing dividends plus price growth is a powerful combo over time.
  • You want quality without picking stocks: every holding has raised its dividend for at least a decade, and the riskiest high yielders are screened out.
  • You invest in a taxable account: low yield, mostly qualified dividends and a 0.04% fee keep the drag small.

Where I'd push back: if you need income now, $VIG is the wrong tool. A 1.5% yield won't pay your bills. I'd rather own $SCHD if I wanted a dividend core today, and add $VIG for quality and growth. If you're early on, the reverse can make total sense.

VIG dividend FAQ

How often does VIG pay dividends?

$VIG pays quarterly, with ex-dividend dates in late March, June, September and December. The cash usually lands two to three business days after the ex-date.

How much does VIG pay per share?

$VIG paid about $3.65 per share over the last 12 months. In 2026 the quarterly payments have been $0.8334 in March, $0.9988 in June and $0.9295 in September.

When is the next VIG dividend?

The next $VIG ex-dividend date should land around December 21, 2026, based on the December 22, 2025 and December 23, 2024 ex-dates. Vanguard announces the exact date and amount right before it happens.

How much will $10,000 in VIG pay?

At a yield of about 1.5%, $10,000 in $VIG pays roughly $150 a year, or about $37.50 a quarter. The real point of $VIG is that the payout grew about 6% a year from 2022 to 2025.

Are VIG dividends qualified?

Mostly, yes. $VIG holds U.S. stocks and leaves out REITs, so the large majority of its dividends are usually qualified and taxed at long-term capital gains rates if you meet the holding period. Vanguard publishes the exact percentage each tax season, and your 1099-DIV is the final word.
