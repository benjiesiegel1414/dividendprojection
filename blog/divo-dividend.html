The Amplify CWP Enhanced Dividend Income ETF launched on December 14, 2016. It's run by Capital Wealth Planning, and the idea is simple: own a short list of high quality, dividend growing blue chips, and sell covered calls on some of them when the premium is worth it.

That makes $DIVO a bit of a middle child. It pays more than a classic dividend growth fund like $SCHD, but a lot less than option income funds like $JEPI or $SPYI. I think that's exactly why people keep searching for it, so let's look at the real numbers.

Why DIVO's December 2025 dividend was so big

If you look at any $DIVO dividend history, one number jumps off the page: $0.95339 a share on December 30, 2025. That's about five times a normal month.

It wasn't a new normal. Funds like $DIVO sometimes pay an extra year-end distribution to clear out what they need to distribute for the year. $DIVO did something similar in December 2019, when it paid a separate $0.85911 on top of its regular $0.14493. In 2020 through 2024 there was no extra payment at all.

Amplify's Rule 19a-1 notice for that December 2025 payment estimated, on a book basis, that 94% of it was return of capital. That's an estimate, not the final tax answer, which comes on your 1099.

Here's why this matters for you: that one check inflates the "trailing 12 month" yield you'll see on a lot of sites. StockAnalysis, for example, shows $3.03 a share over the last year and a 6.88% yield, because the $0.95 December payment is still in the window. Amplify's own distribution rate is 4.85% (as of October 31, 2026), and the TopDividendETFsPRO database shows 4.9%. For planning, I'd use something close to those, not 6.9%, and treat any December extra as a bonus.

The honest read: from 2021 to 2023 the payout went sideways, around $1.71 to $1.83 a year. Then it picked up. 2024 was up about 11%, the regular months of 2025 kept climbing, and 2026 is running about 9% ahead again.

This is not a fund built to grow the dividend every single year like $DGRO or $VIG. Part of the income is option premium, and CWP writes calls tactically, so in some years there's more of it and in some years less. But the trend over the last three years has been up, which is the direction I want.

How DIVO works: dividends plus tactical covered calls

$DIVO has two income streams, according to Amplify:

  • Dividends. It owns about 20 to 25 large cap companies with a history of dividend and earnings growth, screened on things like market cap, management track record, earnings, cash flow and return on equity.
  • Covered calls, written tactically. CWP sells call options on individual stocks it owns, when it thinks the premium is attractive. It does not write calls on the whole portfolio all the time.

This is the part most people miss. Funds like $JEPI or $XYLD sell calls systematically, every month, and that's why they pay so much and give up so much upside. $DIVO sells less, so it pays less, but it keeps more of the stocks' growth. The fund is meant to be a total return fund with income, not an income machine.

The tradeoff is concentration. With only 20 to 25 stocks, a couple of bad picks can matter, and the fund is classified as non-diversified. And when the market rips higher, stocks that had calls written on them can get called away, which caps a bit of the gain.

How DIVO dividends are taxed

$DIVO's monthly payments can be a mix of income, capital gains and return of capital, and Amplify notes the mix can change during the year. The final breakdown shows up on your 1099-DIV.

Return of capital isn't taxed when you get it. It lowers your cost basis, so you pay when you sell. That's what most of the December 2025 extra was estimated to be. The rest of the regular income is a mix that depends on the year.

One clue worth knowing: Amplify lists a 30-day SEC yield of just 1.35% (as of October 31, 2026), versus the 4.85% distribution rate. The SEC yield only counts net investment income from dividends and interest, so the gap tells you a large part of the payout comes from option premium and gains, not from stock dividends alone. For a fund like this, I usually like an IRA or Roth so none of it matters. Talk to a tax pro about your own situation.

My take: who DIVO is for

I've been building dividend income for more than ten years, and I reached financial freedom at 31 doing it. The funds I trust most are the ones where the check is steady and the share price holds up. $DIVO checks both boxes so far.

  • If you want monthly income without giving up most of the growth: $DIVO is one of the better balanced options. About 5% income, about 12.5% a year total return since launch.
  • If you want the highest monthly income: this isn't it. $JEPI, $QDVO and $SPYI all pay more.
  • If fees are your top priority: 0.56% is real money next to $SCHD. On $100,000, that's $560 a year vs $60.
  • If you want broad diversification: remember it's 20 to 25 stocks with very little big tech growth exposure.

What I'd avoid is judging $DIVO on the trailing yield. Strip out the December 2025 bump and you're looking at about a 5% payer with a growing monthly check. That's the honest number. And to compare it with everything else, I built TopDividendETFsPRO.

DIVO dividend FAQ

Does DIVO pay monthly dividends?

Yes. $DIVO pays every month, usually going ex-dividend a day or two before month end and paying the next business day. In 2026 the regular payments have ranged from $0.17872 to $0.19468 a share.

What is the DIVO ETF dividend yield?

$DIVO's dividend yield is about 4.9% in the TopDividendETFsPRO database, which updates daily, and Amplify lists a 4.85% distribution rate as of October 31, 2026. Some sites show close to 7% because they include the one-time $0.95 December 2025 payment.

When is the next DIVO dividend?

The next $DIVO dividend goes ex-dividend November 27, 2026 and is paid November 30, 2026. Because November 26 is Thanksgiving, you would need to buy by the close on November 25 to receive it. The December payment goes ex-dividend December 30 and is paid December 31, 2026.

Why was DIVO's December 2025 dividend so high?

$DIVO paid $0.95339 a share on December 30, 2025, about five times a normal month, as a year-end distribution. Amplify's 19a-1 notice estimated about 94% of it was return of capital on a book basis. It was a one-time bump, not a new monthly level.

Is DIVO a good investment for income?

It depends on your goals. $DIVO holds 20 to 25 dividend growing blue chips, writes covered calls tactically, pays about 5% monthly and returned 12.54% a year at NAV from its December 2016 launch through October 2026. The tradeoffs are a 0.56% fee, concentration and less big tech exposure. Not financial advice.
