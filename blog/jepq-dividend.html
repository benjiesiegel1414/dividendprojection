The JPMorgan Nasdaq Equity Premium Income ETF launched on May 3, 2022, and it's become one of the biggest income ETFs on the market. People love it for one reason: it gives you exposure to the big Nasdaq tech names and pays you a fat check every single month.

But the check is never the same twice. That's the part that confuses a lot of people, so let's walk through the actual numbers.

Notice the January payment. $JEPQ's "January" dividend actually goes ex-dividend at the very end of December. So if you count by ex-date, there are two in December and none in January. Counted by the day the cash arrives, you get one every month.

When is the next JEPQ dividend?

The pattern is extremely consistent. $JEPQ goes ex-dividend on the first business day of the month and pays two to four business days later. November 1, 2026 is a Sunday, so the next ex-date should be Monday, November 2, 2026, with payment around November 4.

After that, expect one around December 1 and another in the last few trading days of December for the January check. J.P. Morgan announces the exact amount the business day before each ex-date, so nobody knows the November number yet. Anyone who tells you otherwise is guessing.

So far the trend is up. The annual payout went from $5.00 in 2023 to $6.12 in 2025, and 2026 is running well ahead of 2025. The first nine payments this year add up to $5.28 a share, versus $4.52 for the same nine months last year.

Here's the honest part though. This is not dividend growth the way $SCHD or $DGRO do it. Those funds raise their payouts because the companies inside raise theirs. $JEPQ's payout rises and falls mostly with how much the option market is paying, which is a very different engine. Don't extrapolate 17% a year into the future.

Why JEPQ's dividend changes every month

To understand the check, you have to understand where the money comes from. $JEPQ has two parts:

  • A stock portfolio picked from the Nasdaq-100. The biggest holdings look a lot like $QQQ, just a bit less concentrated, with the managers leaning toward names they see as less volatile or better valued.
  • Equity-linked notes (ELNs), up to 20% of the fund. These notes sell call options on the Nasdaq-100 and pass the option premium back to the fund. That premium is most of what you get paid.

Option premium depends on volatility. When the market is nervous, call options get expensive and $JEPQ collects more. When things are calm, premiums shrink and so does the monthly check. That's why you see a $0.47 payment in February and a $0.70 payment in August of the same year.

This is the part most people miss: the tradeoff for that income is upside. Selling calls means you hand over some of the gains when tech rips higher. In a strong bull market $JEPQ will lag $QQQ, and that's by design, not a flaw.

The good news is it hasn't eaten itself alive. The TopDividendETFsPRO database shows price decay: none for $JEPQ, and a total return of +92% since launch with dividends reinvested. That's a big difference from older covered call funds like $QYLD, which has paid a similar yield while the share price slid for years.

How JEPQ dividends are taxed, and what the SEC yield means

Short version: most of $JEPQ's income is not qualified. The option premium that comes through the ELNs is generally taxed as ordinary income, at the same rate as your paycheck. A small piece from the stocks' own dividends can be qualified, but it's a minor slice of the total.

That's why a lot of income investors hold $JEPQ in an IRA or Roth and keep their qualified dividend payers, like $SCHD, in taxable accounts. If you hold it in a regular brokerage account, check Box 1a versus 1b on your 1099-DIV to see the exact split for your shares.

A lot of people also search for the "JEPQ SEC yield." The 30-day SEC yield is a standardized number that mostly counts dividends and interest from the holdings, minus expenses. It doesn't capture the option premium the way the distribution does, so it shows up far lower than the 11% to 12% you actually get paid. Neither number is wrong. They just measure different things. For a monthly income fund, I look at the trailing 12-month payout and the total return together.

My take: who JEPQ is for

I've been building dividend income for over a decade, and funds like $JEPQ changed the game for people who want high monthly income without giving up on tech completely.

  • If you're living off your portfolio: $JEPQ is one of the more proven ways to turn Nasdaq exposure into monthly cash, with no price decay so far. Just budget for the check moving around. For the cash you can't afford to see swing, a T-bill fund works better; here's how much $SGOV pays per month.
  • If you're early and building: reinvest the payouts, and know that a plain growth fund will likely beat it in a long bull market. Many people pair a core like $SCHD or $VOO with a slice of $JEPQ.
  • If you hold it in a taxable account: remember most of the income is taxed as ordinary income. An IRA is usually the better home.

What I'd avoid is judging $JEPQ by one month's payment, or chasing whatever option fund has the highest yield this week. Look at the 12-month payout, the total return and price decay together. JEPQ dividend FAQ

How often does JEPQ pay dividends?

$JEPQ pays monthly, 12 times a year. The ex-dividend date is usually the first business day of the month, and the cash lands two to four business days later. The January payment usually goes ex-dividend on the last trading days of December.

When is the next JEPQ dividend payout?

The next $JEPQ dividend is expected to go ex-dividend around Monday, November 2, 2026, the first business day of November, with payment a few days later. J.P. Morgan announces the exact amount the business day before the ex-date. The most recent payment was $0.56687 per share, ex-dividend October 1 and paid October 5, 2026.

What is JEPQ's dividend yield?

$JEPQ's dividend yield is currently about 11.6%, based on the TopDividendETFsPRO database, which updates daily. It paid $6.89 per share over the last 12 months.

Are JEPQ dividends qualified?

Mostly no. The bulk of $JEPQ's income comes from option premium paid through equity-linked notes, which is generally taxed as ordinary income. Only a small slice from the stocks' own dividends tends to be qualified. Your 1099-DIV shows the exact split, and many investors hold $JEPQ in an IRA for this reason.

How much does $10,000 in JEPQ pay per month?

At the current yield, $10,000 in $JEPQ pays about $96.67 a month on average, or $1,160 a year. The actual monthly check moves up and down because the option income changes with market volatility.
