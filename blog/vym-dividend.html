The Vanguard High Dividend Yield ETF launched on November 10, 2006. It's one of the oldest and biggest dividend ETFs out there, with over $80 billion in it, and it's the fund a lot of people land on when they want "more dividends than the S&P 500, but still cheap and diversified."

The name makes it sound like a high-yield fund. It isn't, really. At about 2.3%, it pays roughly double what $VOO pays, but well under $SCHD. So let's look at the real numbers and figure out where it actually fits.

Notice the amounts move around a bit from quarter to quarter. That's normal. $VYM passes through whatever its 600 or so companies paid during the quarter, and companies don't all pay on the same calendar. June tends to be a little bigger, and December often is too.

When is the next VYM dividend?

Vanguard has kept $VYM on a steady rhythm: ex-dividend dates land in the second half of March, June, September and December. The last two December ex-dates were December 20, 2024 and December 19, 2025. So the next one should be around December 18, 2026, with the cash landing a few days before Christmas.

Vanguard announces the exact date and amount right before the ex-date, so the December number isn't known yet. Based on the last few years, I'd expect something in the $0.85 to $1.00 range, but that's a guess, not a promise. Dividends from a stock fund can go down as well as up.

If you're buying for the dividend, remember the share price drops by about the dividend amount on the ex-date. Buying the day before to "grab" the payment doesn't make you money. It just turns part of your investment into a taxable distribution.

This is the part most people miss about $VYM. From 2023 through 2025 the dividend basically went sideways: $3.48, $3.49, $3.50. That's not a dividend growth fund. It's a "pay me a decent yield and don't do anything weird" fund.

2026 looks better. The first three payments add up to $2.73 a share, versus $2.55 for the same three quarters of 2025. That's about 6.8% more income on the same shares. Whether that holds depends on the December check, but it's a nice change of pace.

If dividend growth is what you're after, a fund like $DGRO or $VIG is built for that, and both start with a lower yield. With $VYM you're trading growth speed for more income today.

What VYM holds (and why the yield isn't higher)

$VYM tracks the FTSE High Dividend Yield Index. The idea is simple: take U.S. stocks, rank them by forecast dividend yield, and keep the top half or so, weighted by market cap. REITs are left out. As of September 30, 2026, that came to 603 holdings.

See the number one holding? $AVGO is almost 7% of the fund, and Broadcom is not what most people picture when they hear "high dividend yield." Because the index is weighted by market cap, the biggest dividend payers by size end up on top, even if their own yield is modest. That's exactly why $VYM's overall yield sits around 2.3% instead of 4%.

I actually think that's a feature, not a bug. It means $VYM isn't loaded up on struggling companies with high yields because their stock price collapsed. You get banks, energy, healthcare, industrials and some tech, spread across hundreds of names. The TopDividendETFsPRO database shows price decay: none and a total return of +492% since launch with dividends reinvested.

The fee is tiny too. At 0.04%, you pay about $4 a year on $10,000.

Are VYM dividends qualified? How they're taxed

For the most part, yes. $VYM holds U.S. common stocks and skips REITs, so the large majority of its dividends usually count as qualified dividends. In a taxable account those are taxed at the lower long-term capital gains rates (0%, 15% or 20% depending on your income), as long as you've held the shares more than 60 days around the ex-date.

It's usually not 100%, though. Small slices can come from sources that don't qualify. Vanguard publishes the qualified percentage for each fund every tax season, and your 1099-DIV is the final word for your account.

That's a real edge over interest-based income. A T-bill fund like $SGOV or a covered call fund like $JEPI pays income that's mostly taxed at ordinary rates. In a taxable account, a 2.3% qualified yield can hold up better after tax than it looks on paper.

My take: who VYM is for

I've been a dividend investor for over a decade, and $VYM is one of those funds I respect more the longer I look at it. It's not exciting. It's not supposed to be.

  • You want a core dividend fund that won't blow up: 600 stocks, a 0.04% fee and no price decay. Hard to mess this up.
  • You want more income than the S&P 500: roughly double $VOO's yield, without going all in on covered call funds.
  • You invest in a taxable account: mostly qualified dividends make the income more tax friendly than bond or option income.

Where I'd push back: don't buy $VYM expecting fast dividend growth. The payout went flat for three years before picking up in 2026. And if income is the whole goal, $SCHD has paid more with a better long-run total return. I'd rather own $SCHD as my main dividend fund and use something like $VYM for broader diversification, but plenty of investors do the opposite and do just fine.

VYM dividend FAQ

How often does VYM pay dividends?

$VYM pays quarterly, with ex-dividend dates in the second half of March, June, September and December. The cash usually arrives two to four business days after the ex-date.

How much does VYM pay per share?

$VYM paid $3.68 per share over the last 12 months. In 2026 the quarterly payments have been $0.8617 in March, $0.9795 in June and $0.8869 in September.

When is the next VYM dividend?

The next $VYM ex-dividend date should be around December 18, 2026, based on the December 19, 2025 and December 20, 2024 ex-dates. Vanguard announces the exact date and amount just before it happens.

What is VYM's dividend yield?

$VYM's yield is about 2.3% based on the TopDividendETFsPRO database, which updates daily. That means $10,000 pays roughly $230 a year.

Are VYM dividends qualified?

Mostly, yes. $VYM holds U.S. stocks and excludes REITs, so the large majority of its dividends are usually qualified and taxed at long-term capital gains rates if you meet the holding period. Vanguard publishes the exact qualified percentage each tax season, and your 1099-DIV is the final word.
