The ProShares S&P 500 Dividend Aristocrats ETF launched on October 9, 2013. It's the fund most people mean when they say "dividend aristocrats ETF": it owns the S&P 500 companies that have raised their dividend for at least 25 years in a row, and ProShares says it's the only ETF focused exclusively on that list.

I like the idea behind $NOBL a lot. A company doesn't raise its dividend through 25 years of recessions, rate hikes and a pandemic by accident. But the idea and the actual income you get are two different things, so let's look at the real numbers.

Why NOBL's dividend looks smaller in 2026: the 2-for-1 split

If you own $NOBL and your September check looked about half the size of last year's, nothing got cut. ProShares announced on May 11, 2026 that $NOBL would split 2-for-1, effective before the open on May 28, 2026. Every share became two shares, each worth about half as much.

So the March 2026 dividend, which was paid on the old, bigger shares, works out to $0.25612 per new share. The total value of your position didn't change and neither did your income. You just have twice as many shares getting half as much each.

Every per-share number in this guide is split-adjusted. One heads up: ProShares' own distribution table shows amounts that are not adjusted for later splits, so older payments there will look about twice as big. If you're comparing to an older article or your old statements, divide the pre-June 2026 numbers by two.

From 2022 to 2025, the payout grew from $0.872 to $1.113, about 8.5% a year. That's the whole point of an aristocrats fund, and it's faster than $HDV's roughly 2.8% a year over a similar stretch. It's not a straight line though: 2024 only grew 2.6% after a big 2023.

Here's the part most people miss. Every company in the fund raised its dividend, but the fund's payout doesn't have to rise every year. $NOBL rebalances back to roughly equal weights, it swaps companies in and out, and it trims winners. The checks you get depend on all of that, not just on the raises.

For the current yield, the cleanest numbers come from ProShares: its fund page listed a 30-day SEC yield of 2.28% and a 12-month yield of 2.17% as of September 30, 2026.

How NOBL picks its stocks

$NOBL tracks the S&P 500 Dividend Aristocrats Index. ProShares sums up the rules simply:

  • S&P 500 members only. No small caps, no mid caps, no foreign stocks. That's narrower than $SDY, which draws from the S&P 1500 and only needs 20 years of raises.
  • 25+ years of dividend increases. Not just paying, but raising the dividend every single year. If fewer than 40 stocks qualify, the index can add companies with shorter streaks.
  • Cut and you're out. A company that reduces or eliminates its dividend gets dropped at the next reconstitution. Special dividends don't count.
  • Roughly equal weights. You can see it in the holdings: on November 18, 2026, the biggest position was 1.83% and the smallest was 1.07%. No single stock can dominate.

That equal weighting is the big difference from almost every other dividend ETF. In $HDV, Exxon alone is almost 9% of the fund. In $NOBL, Exxon is 1.27%, the same ballpark as Hormel or Clorox.

Scroll further down the list and it reads like a who's who of boring, durable American companies: Procter & Gamble, Coca-Cola, PepsiCo, Johnson & Johnson, Walmart, Colgate-Palmolive, Chevron, ExxonMobil, Caterpillar, IBM and Realty Income, among others.

What you won't find is the big tech crowd. No Apple, no Microsoft, no Nvidia. Most of today's mega caps either don't pay a dividend or haven't been raising one for 25 years. One tax note: a few holdings are REITs, like Realty Income, Federal Realty and Essex Property Trust. REIT dividends usually aren't qualified, so not every dollar from $NOBL gets the lower qualified dividend rate. Most of it comes from regular U.S. companies, but check Box 1b of your 1099-DIV for the exact split, and talk to a tax pro about your situation.

The tradeoffs: what you give up with NOBL

  • A higher fee. 0.35% is more than four times $DGRO's 0.08% and almost six times $SCHD's 0.06%. It compounds against you every year.
  • A modest yield. Around 2.2%. If you need income now, this won't get you there alone.
  • Almost no big tech. That cushions you when tech crashes and costs you when tech leads, which is a big reason the 5-year return is only about 7% a year.
  • Backward-looking rules. A 25-year streak tells you what a company did, not what it will do. Companies that cut get dropped, but usually after the damage to the price is done.

None of these are deal breakers. They're just the price of owning a list of very steady companies in equal slices.

My take: who NOBL is for

I've been building dividend income for more than ten years, and the companies in $NOBL are exactly the kind I like to own: boring, profitable, and committed to raising the check. The fund itself is a different question, and here's how I'd think about it:

  • If you want a simple, quality dividend growth fund: $NOBL does that job, with real payout growth of about 8.5% a year from 2022 to 2025 and no single stock dominating.
  • If cost matters most to you: I'd rather own $SCHD or $DGRO. Similar idea, a fraction of the fee.
  • If your portfolio is mostly an S&P 500 fund: $NOBL is a genuine diversifier, because it owns the dividend payers that market-cap funds barely notice.
  • If you need high income today: look elsewhere. A 2% yield is about growing income over decades.

NOBL dividend FAQ

How often does NOBL pay dividends?

Quarterly. $NOBL pays four times a year, in late March, June, September and December. For 2026, ProShares scheduled the ex-dividend dates for March 25, June 24, September 23 and December 23.

When is the next NOBL dividend?

According to ProShares' 2026 distribution schedule, the next $NOBL dividend goes ex-dividend December 23, 2026 and is paid December 30, 2026. The amount is announced around the ex-date.

Why did NOBL's dividend drop in 2026?

It didn't. $NOBL split 2-for-1 effective May 28, 2026, so each share is worth about half as much and gets about half the dividend. Split-adjusted, the 2026 payments are running about 7.7% ahead of the same three quarters in 2025.

What is NOBL's dividend yield?

$NOBL's dividend yield is currently about 2.0%, based on the TopDividendETFsPRO database, which updates daily. It paid about $1.17 a share over the last 12 months, and ProShares listed a 30-day SEC yield of 2.28% as of September 30, 2026.

Is NOBL a good dividend ETF?

It holds about 70 S&P 500 companies that have raised their dividends for 25+ years, in roughly equal weights, and returned 10.95% a year at NAV over the 10 years to October 31, 2026. The tradeoffs are a 0.35% fee, a yield around 2% and almost no big tech. Whether it fits depends on your goals. Not financial advice.
