About DGRO

DGRO tracks the Morningstar US Dividend Growth Index, which looks for companies with at least five years of uninterrupted dividend growth and screens out payers whose dividends look stretched compared with their earnings. The highest-yielding names are left out as well.

With close to 400 holdings, DGRO is one of the broadest dividend growth funds available. Financials, technology, health care, consumer staples and industrials make up most of the portfolio, so it sits between a pure dividend fund and the overall market.

It costs very little to own and pays quarterly. The shorter five-year growth requirement, compared with VIG's ten, lets DGRO include companies earlier in their dividend growth story.

DGRO FAQ

What is DGRO's dividend yield?

DGRO's dividend yield is currently 2.0%, based on the TopDividendETFsPRO database, which is updated daily.

How often does DGRO pay dividends?

DGRO pays quarterly.

How much does $10,000 in DGRO pay per month?

At the current yield, about $16.67 a month on average, or $200 a year, assuming the yield stays the same.

How much do I need in DGRO to make $1,000 a month?

About $600,000 at the current yield.

What is DGRO's total return?

DGRO has returned +310% since it launched in 2014, with dividends reinvested, about +12.2% a year on average.

How is DGRO different from VIG?

DGRO requires five years of dividend growth and holds close to 400 stocks. VIG requires ten years and removes the top 25% highest yielders.
